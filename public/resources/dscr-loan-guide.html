DSCR Loan Guide for Real Estate Investors

How Debt Service Coverage Ratio loans work, who they're best for, and how we use them to help investors scale their St. Louis portfolios.

What Is a DSCR Loan?

A DSCR loan — Debt Service Coverage Ratio loan — is an investment property mortgage that qualifies you based on the property's rental income rather than your personal income. The lender asks one question: does the rent cover the mortgage?

This makes DSCR loans the preferred financing tool for:

  • Self-employed investors whose tax returns don't reflect their actual income
  • Investors who already have multiple mortgages and can't add another conventional loan
  • Investors who want to scale quickly without hitting Fannie Mae's 10-loan limit
  • Investors who want to keep personal and investment finances cleanly separated
Bottom line: If a property qualifies, you qualify. Your W-2s, pay stubs, tax returns, and personal debt load don't factor in.

How DSCR Is Calculated

DSCR measures the ratio of a property's gross rental income to its total monthly debt service (PITIA: Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance, and HOA if applicable).

Example Calculation

Monthly gross rent$1,150
Monthly principal + interest$698
Monthly taxes$110
Monthly insurance$75
Total PITIA (debt service)$883
DSCR = $1,150 ÷ $8831.30 ✓

Most lenders require a DSCR of 1.20 or higher to approve the loan. Some lenders go as low as 1.0 (break-even) but at higher rates. A DSCR below 1.0 means the rent doesn't cover the mortgage — most lenders won't touch this.

Pros & Cons vs. Conventional Investment Loans

Advantages

  • No personal income verification
  • No W-2 or tax return requirements
  • No limit on number of properties
  • Works for LLCs and entities
  • Faster closing (often 2–3 weeks)
  • Scale without hitting DTI limits

Trade-offs

  • Higher interest rate (0.5–1.5% above conventional)
  • Higher down payment often required
  • Stricter reserve requirements
  • Property must cash flow at 1.2x+
  • Prepayment penalties common (3–5 years)
  • Not eligible for primary residence

DSCR in the BRRRR Strategy

DSCR loans are the financing engine that makes the BRRRR strategy work at scale. Here's how:

  1. Buy: Purchase distressed property with cash or hard money
  2. Rehab: Renovate to increase value to ARV
  3. Rent: Place a tenant at market rent
  4. Refinance: Use DSCR loan at 75% of new appraised ARV to pull out capital
  5. Repeat: Recycled capital funds the next deal
Example: All-in cost $110,000. ARV $155,000. DSCR refi at 75% LTV = $116,250 loan. You get $116,250 back — more than you put in — while still owning a cash-flowing asset with $38,750 in equity.

Questions to Ask a DSCR Lender

  • What is your minimum DSCR ratio?
  • What rent figure do you use — actual lease, market rent appraisal, or Section 8 schedule?
  • Do you lend to LLCs, and what are the additional requirements?
  • What is the prepayment penalty structure?
  • What are your reserve requirements?
  • What is your typical closing timeline?
  • Do you have a minimum loan amount?
  • How do you handle properties with Section 8 tenants?
